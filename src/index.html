Glossary

Size and memory

Parameters
The size of the model's learned "brain," in billions (B) or trillions (T) of settings it picked up in training. Bigger usually knows more but costs more and runs slower. "Active" means how much of it works on each word; models built this way run cheaper for their size. OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and xAI don't publish sizes, so those show Small, Medium, or Large.
Context
How much the model can read and keep in mind at once: your instructions, files, and the conversation so far. 1M (1 million) tokens is about 750,000 words, or a few long books. Size isn't the same as using it well; see "Long-context accuracy."
Token
The unit models read and write in, about ¾ of a word. All prices here are per 1 million tokens.

Cost

Input
What you pay for everything you send the model: your question, instructions, and any files.
Cached input
A cheaper input rate for text the model saw recently, such as the same long instructions sent every time. Agents that repeat a long setup save the most here.
Output
What you pay for everything the model writes back, including its hidden "thinking" on reasoning models. Output usually costs 4 to 8 times more than input.
Typical cost
One number to compare models: 3 parts input to 1 part output, a common mix for chat and agent work.
Coding per $
Coding score divided by typical cost. Higher means more coding skill for each dollar.
Subscription-only tools
Image and video apps you pay for by the month instead of by the token, such as Midjourney, Runway, and Descript. Turn on Show subscription-only tools in the filters to list them. They have no test scores here, and their prices and notes come from each maker's pricing page as of the date shown at the top.
Image quality (Elo)
A score from blind votes where people pick the better of two images made from the same prompt (Artificial Analysis Image Arena). Higher is better; 1000 is the baseline. It rates overall quality, not one style. Shows only on the image projects.
Free to download
The model's files are public, so you can run it free on your own computer with a tool like ComfyUI, if your computer is powerful enough.
Helpful tools
Outside tools people often use with AI for each project type, listed below the table. Setup shows how much work each takes: Easy, Some setup, or Technical. Prices come from each tool's pricing page as of the date at the top.
Where to buy
"OpenRouter" means you can use it through one OpenRouter account. "Maker only" means you buy it from the company that made it.

Scores (0 to 100, higher is better)

Coding
Average of two tests: Terminal-Bench, where the model works on its own in a computer terminal to finish real coding jobs, and SciCode, which asks it to write code for science problems.
GPQA
A graduate-level science quiz in biology, physics, and chemistry, written so answers can't be looked up. PhD experts score about 65.
Smarts
The Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index: one score that blends ten hard tests in math, science, coding, and reasoning. It replaces MMLU, which testing sites retired because top models all score near the top.

Reliability (0 to 100; higher is better, except hallucination rate)

Reliability
How safely you can hand the model a long job and trust the result. It blends three things: a low hallucination rate, long-context accuracy, and coding. A model needs all three to get a reliability score.
Fact accuracy
The share of thousands of hard fact questions the model answers correctly (AA-Omniscience test). Higher is better. Read it with hallucination rate: accuracy shows how often it knows; hallucination rate shows what it does when it doesn't.
Hallucination rate
When the model doesn't know an answer, how often it makes one up instead of saying "I don't know" (AA-Omniscience test, thousands of hard fact questions). Correct answers don't count. Lower is better: 90% means that when it's stuck, it bluffs 9 times out of 10. Most models score high here; under 50% is good.
Long-context accuracy
How well it finds and reasons over facts buried in long documents of 10,000 to 100,000 tokens, such as a stack of reports or a big spreadsheet (AA-LCR test). Higher is better.
Tool use
How well it finishes multi-step customer-service jobs that need tools, while a simulated customer talks back (τ²-Bench test). Newer models often have no score here because the testing site stopped running it.
Follows rules
How well it sticks to exact instructions, such as "answer in 3 bullets" or "don't use the word very" (IFBench test). Newer models often have no score here for the same reason.
A dash (–)
No independent test result yet. It doesn't mean the model scored zero.
